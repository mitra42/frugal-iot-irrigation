Key choices, why they were made, and what would reopen them. Status: **Decided** (go ahead), **Proposed**
(default unless changed), **Open**. "Irrigation D73" means a decision in
`../irrigation-layout-oct-2026/DECISIONS.md`, where most of the valve-driver reasoning lives.

## 21 Nov 2026 — the irrigation board as an add-on for the sensor and LoRa boards (Mitra)

| # | Decision | Status | Why / revisit if |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **An add-on board with only the valve drivers and an OLED header**, driven over I2C by a Frugal-IoT sensor board (frugal-iot-hw-sensor V0.2) or LoRa board (frugal-iot-hw-lora V0.3), instead of a complete irrigation board with its own ESP32. **No 12 V → 5 V buck**: the host is powered from the USB port most MPPT charge controllers have. **No RS485 here** | Decided (Mitra) | The host boards are made in larger numbers, so the expensive half (module, charger, USB) rides on their volume and this board is cheap (D7). RS485 soil probes are deferred until there is experimental evidence: the hosts only provide 3V3_SW on their RS485 terminal, most probes need 5–30 V, and the RS485 lines never come near this board. Revisit with that evidence — e.g. a switched 12 V output for the probes on one expander pin |
| D2 | **Host connection: J1, a 5-way XY308 (2.54 mm) screw terminal, wired by a short cable to the host's J2.** Read left to right on the board's top edge J1 is GND, SDA, SCL, 3V3_SW, V12 — the host's J2 pins 1, 2, 3, 4 and 9 read top to bottom; V12_SENSE goes to host J2.9 (SENS2). Pin names on the silkscreen. In the footprint's own numbering that is pin 5 = GND … pin 1 = V12 | Decided (Mitra); order reversed 22 Nov 2026 | XY308 is the part the hosts use, so it costs no extra feeder fee family-wide. A cable lets the two boards sit anywhere in the enclosure (the host in its 40 mm pipe). A stacking header would need a host revision. The order was first GND … V12 by footprint pin number, which reads backwards along the edge (the terminal faces the edge, so its pin 1 is on the right): a cable between the two boards side by side then had to twist, and a wiring diagram needed ten crossings. Re-routed; DRC still clean |
| D3 | **All logic on this board is powered from the host's 3V3_SW** (switched sensor rail): the TCA9534 (U1) and the OLED. I2C pull-ups R19/R20 here too (D12). U1 at 0x20 (A0–A2 to GND) | Decided | Fail-safe: when the host turns the rail off, U1 is unpowered, its pins go high-impedance and the 100k pull-downs turn every valve off — the same thing that holds them off at power-up. The irrigation board put its expander on always-on 3V3 to hold relays through a deep sleep; that is not needed, because the firmware refuses to sleep while a cycle runs (`Control_Irrigation::okToSleep`). Firmware must switch the rail on before any I2C, as on the irrigation board. Revisit (add address jumpers) for two add-ons on one host: 0x20 is fixed |
| D4 | **12 V battery reading for the host: R1 560k / R2 100k to J1.5 → host SENS2** (GPIO1 on the sensor board's C3, GPIO5 on the LoRa board's S3, both ADC1; each host already has a 100 nF filter fitted on SENS2). 2.27 V at 15 V, the irrigation board's divider (x6.6) | Decided (Mitra) | The irrigation firmware's low-voltage interlock (the power blocker that closes valves on a flat battery) needs the 12 V battery, and the hosts only measure their own LiPo. Uses SENS2 and a fifth wire. Draws ~18 µA from the battery all the time; with the host unpowered ~15 µA flows into SENS2's ESD diode — harmless. Firmware: `SENSOR_BATTERY_PIN` = the host's SENS2 GPIO, `SENSOR_BATTERY_VOLTAGE_DIVIDER=6.6` |
| D5 | **12 V input and valve channels exactly as the irrigation board**: J2 KF128 → F1 3 A/16 V PTC → D1 SS54 → D2 SMAJ15A, C1 10 µF bulk at the +12V feed (irrigation D77); eight channels of AO3400A low-side, 100k pull-down, SS34 flyback to the valve's own +12V, red LED + 4.7k, KF128 terminal (irrigation D73, D75). The same `valve_route.py` copper: 2 mm input path, 2 mm +12V bus on B.Cu along the terminal row, 1 mm per valve, two GND vias per FET source (irrigation D82) | Decided | Proven reasoning, nothing about it depends on what drives the gates. ~2 A per valve, ~3 A in total (F1); 12 V DC valves only — not latching, not 24 V AC |
| D6 | **Board 100 × 42 mm**, 2 layers, 4 × M3 corner holes. The valve band along the bottom edge as the irrigation board (eight butted terminals, one column of parts above each); 12 V input on the left edge above it; J1 (host) and J3 (OLED, the irrigation board's J9 pinout: GND, VCC, SCL, SDA) on the top edge; U1 centred above the band. Title "Frugal-IoT Irrigation Daughter V0.1" | Decided — title open | 42 mm is the least height at which J2 clears the top-left mounting hole (`place.py` now checks every part against the holes). Width is set by the 81.3 mm terminal row plus the bottom corner holes. Routed in two Freerouting rounds |
| D7 | **Cost, JLCPCB prices fetched 21 Nov 2026: $8.21/board at 10, $4.46 at 100** (Economic; 15 part numbers, 5 Extended). **With a sensor board ($11.51 / $7.16): $19.72 / $11.62** — a little under the irrigation board's $21.04 / $12.07, which also had RS485-probe power, a buck and a second regulator. With a LoRa board ($17.31 / $11.58): $25.52 / $16.04. The pull-ups (D12) added a cent. Bare PCB **estimated** from the irrigation board's quote, scaled by area at 100 | Decided | Re-quote from `fabrication/gerbers.zip`. The comparison is not like for like: the irrigation board's buck and probe supply are gone here, and the MPPT controller's USB port is assumed. A cable and its labour are extra |
| D8 | **Checks (22 Nov 2026, after D12): DRC 0 violations, 0 unconnected, schematic parity 0; ERC 0.** JLCPCB files: 61 placed parts, BOM and CPL agree | Decided | Not yet through JLCPCB's assembly preview (rotations) |
| D9 | **Ground: valve current returns through J2.2 to the battery; the cable's GND is the logic reference only.** | Open | This assumes the MPPT controller's USB output is referenced to the battery negative (common-negative controllers). On a common-positive controller, which switches the negative, the host's GND and this board's GND are different nodes, the cable ties them, and part of the valve current could flow through the cable and the host. Check the controller before relying on it |
| D10 | **Firmware not yet done**: the irrigation app needs a PlatformIO env for each host — the host's I2C pins, `SYSTEM_POWER3v3_PIN`, `IRRIGATION_RELAY_TCA9534_ADDRESS=0x20` with the valve bits, D4's battery pin, and the OLED on the host's I2C pins (as the `frugal_irrigation_v01` env) | Open | Not started: the boards are not built yet |
| D11 | **Sleep and wake, checked (22 Nov 2026):** when the host deep-sleeps it turns 3V3_SW off, U1 loses power, its pins float and the 100k pull-downs hold every gate low - all valves off. At wake the rail returns and U1 powers up with every pin an input and its output register at 0xFF; the library (`System_TCA9534::configureOutput`) sees the pin is still an input, writes its output bit OFF, and only then makes it an output - so no valve blips on at wake | Decided | Caveat: that check runs in `setup()` only. If the rail were ever cut and restored without a reboot (a light sleep that powers the rail down), U1 would come back as all inputs and stay so: valves safely off, but unable to turn on until a reboot. Not a problem with the irrigation app today, which never sleeps during a cycle |
| D12 | **I2C pull-ups on this board: R19/R20, 4.7k from SDA and SCL to 3V3_SW, FITTED** (22 Nov 2026). The sensor and LoRa boards have 4.7k on 3V3_SW; **a Lolin S2 Mini has none**, and an OLED module's own are there only when an OLED is, which most installations will not have. Next to the 12 V divider (R1/R2) on the top edge | Decided (Mitra) | Fitted rather than left as empty pads: removing a 0603 from a board whose host has its own is easier than adding one, and leaving both in is fine anyway - 4.7k in parallel with the host's 4.7k is ~2.35k, ~1.4 mA pulling down at 3.3 V, well inside I2C's 3 mA; an OLED's as well still leaves it under. Added a cent (D7). Re-placed and re-routed; D8 checks repeated |
